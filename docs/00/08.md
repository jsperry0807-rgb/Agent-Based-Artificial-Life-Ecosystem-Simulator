## Introducing Stochastic Events

Stochastic events are random, unpredictable occurrences that can affect the ecosystem at various scales—individual, population, or environment. They introduce realism by mimicking the role of chance in nature and can reveal how robust or fragile your simulated system is. Below are detailed ideas for incorporating stochastic events into your rabbit–grass model, along with implementation guidelines.

---

### 1. Environmental Shocks (Global or Local)

#### a. **Drought**
- **Description**: A period of reduced rainfall that slows grass regrowth.
- **Implementation**:
  - Trigger with a low probability at each time step (e.g., 1% chance to start a drought, 5% chance to end it).
  - During a drought, multiply `grass_regrowth_time` by a factor (e.g., 2–5) or set a temporary maximum grass regrowth countdown.
  - Could be spatially heterogeneous: some areas experience drought while others do not.
- **Effect**: Reduces food availability, increasing competition and potentially causing population crashes.

#### b. **Fire**
- **Description**: A fire that burns grass in a region, destroying it instantly and possibly harming rabbits.
- **Implementation**:
  - Randomly select a cell as ignition point with a small probability (e.g., 0.1% per step).
  - Spread fire to neighboring cells with some probability (like a cellular automaton).
  - Grass in burned cells is set to `grown=False` and must regrow from zero (maybe with a longer recovery).
  - Rabbits in burned cells could die with a certain probability or take damage (energy loss).
- **Effect**: Creates patchy landscapes, resets succession, and can cause local extinctions.

#### c. **Flood / Heavy Rain**
- **Description**: Excess water that temporarily increases grass growth but may drown rabbits in low‑lying areas.
- **Implementation**:
  - Define a flood probability (e.g., 2% per step).
  - During flood, grass regrowth time is reduced (e.g., by half) for a few steps, but rabbits in cells below a certain “altitude” (if you add elevation) might lose energy or die.
- **Effect**: Boosts food temporarily but adds mortality risk.

#### d. **Cold Spell / Heatwave**
- **Description**: Extreme temperatures that increase rabbit metabolism (more energy consumed) or reduce reproduction.
- **Implementation**:
  - Apply a global modifier to `gene_metabolism` (e.g., multiply by 1.5) for a random duration.
  - Could also lower reproduction threshold or prevent reproduction altogether.
- **Effect**: Increases energy stress, potentially leading to higher mortality.

---

### 2. Demographic Stochasticity

#### a. **Random Death (Non‑energy Related)**
- **Description**: Even healthy rabbits may die from accidents, disease, or old age.
- **Implementation**:
  - At each step, each rabbit has a small fixed probability of death (e.g., 0.5%), independent of energy.
  - Could be age‑dependent if you add an age trait.
- **Effect**: Adds background mortality, preventing populations from growing indefinitely.

#### b. **Reproduction Failure**
- **Description**: Not every mating or reproduction attempt succeeds.
- **Implementation**:
  - When a rabbit meets the reproduction threshold, instead of always producing an offspring, it succeeds with a probability (e.g., 80%).
  - Could also link to environmental conditions (e.g., lower success during drought).
- **Effect**: Introduces randomness in population growth, making booms and busts more realistic.

#### c. **Birth Defects / Litter Size Variation**
- **Description**: Offspring number or traits can vary randomly.
- **Implementation**:
  - Instead of always one offspring, draw litter size from a Poisson or uniform distribution (e.g., 1–3).
  - Offspring energy could also vary randomly (e.g., parent gives half energy, but with some noise).
- **Effect**: Increases genetic diversity and population variability.

---

### 3. Genetic Mutations as Stochastic Events

You already have mutations during reproduction, but you can make them more event‑like:

- **Rare beneficial/harmful mutations**: With a very low probability, a mutation could cause a large change in a trait (e.g., doubling speed) instead of the usual small multiplier.
- **Horizontal gene transfer** (not realistic for rabbits, but could be fun in a microbial model): unlikely here.

---

### 4. Immigration / Emigration

- **Description**: New rabbits occasionally arrive from outside the simulated area, or some rabbits leave.
- **Implementation**:
  - At each step, with low probability, a new rabbit appears at a random edge cell (immigration). Its genes could be drawn from a baseline distribution.
  - Emigration: a rabbit at the edge might leave the grid (remove the agent) with some probability.
- **Effect**: Maintains genetic diversity and can rescue populations from extinction.

---

### 5. Resource Pulses

- **Description**: Sudden abundance of food, e.g., a temporary “grass bloom” after rain.
- **Implementation**:
  - For a short period, all grass regrows instantly each step, or grass energy value is doubled.
  - Trigger randomly or after a drought (as a compensatory effect).
- **Effect**: Creates boom‑bust cycles in rabbit population.

## How to Implement Stochastic Events in Mesa

### General Approach
1. **Define event parameters**: probability, duration, intensity, spatial extent.
2. **In the model’s `step` method**, before agent actions, roll for global events.
3. **Maintain state variables** (e.g., `self.drought_active`, `self.drought_duration_left`) to persist event effects across steps.
4. **Modify agent rules** conditionally based on these state variables.
5. **For spatial events**, you may need to loop over cells or use a separate grid layer.

### Example: Implementing a Drought

```python
class EcosystemModel(mesa.Model):
    def __init__(self, ...):
        # ... existing code ...
        self.drought_active = False
        self.drought_duration = 0
        self.drought_prob = 0.01   # probability per step to start
        self.drought_length_range = (5, 15)

    def step(self):
        # Environmental event phase
        if not self.drought_active:
            if self.random.random() < self.drought_prob:
                self.drought_active = True
                self.drought_duration = self.random.randint(*self.drought_length_range)
        else:
            self.drought_duration -= 1
            if self.drought_duration <= 0:
                self.drought_active = False

        # Modify grass regrowth based on drought
        # (you'll need to pass this info to Grass agents, e.g., via model attribute)
        # Then proceed with normal step: grass regrow, rabbits act
        self.datacollector.collect(self)
        self.agents_by_type[Grass].do("step")
        self.agents_by_type[Rabbit].shuffle_do("step")
```

In the `Grass.step()` method:
```python
def step(self):
    if not self.grown:
        self.countdown -= 1
        regrowth_time = self.model.grass_regrowth_time
        if self.model.drought_active:
            regrowth_time *= 2   # drought slows regrowth
        if self.countdown <= 0:
            self.grown = True
```

### Example: Implementing a Fire (Spatial)

```python
def step(self):
    # ... after collecting data ...
    if self.random.random() < self.fire_prob:
        self.start_fire()

def start_fire(self):
    # Choose random ignition cell
    x = self.random.randrange(self.width)
    y = self.random.randrange(self.height)
    self.burn_cell((x, y), iterations=5)   # fire spreads up to 5 steps

def burn_cell(self, pos, iterations):
    if iterations <= 0:
        return
    # Get grass at pos and destroy it
    cell_agents = self.grid.get_cell_list_contents(pos)
    for agent in cell_agents:
        if isinstance(agent, Grass):
            agent.grown = False
            agent.countdown = self.grass_regrowth_time * 2  # longer recovery after fire
        elif isinstance(agent, Rabbit):
            # 50% chance rabbit dies in fire
            if self.random.random() < 0.5:
                agent.remove()
    # Spread to neighbors with probability
    neighbors = self.grid.get_neighborhood(pos, moore=True, include_center=False)
    for npos in neighbors:
        if self.random.random() < 0.3:   # spread probability
            self.burn_cell(npos, iterations-1)
```

**Important**: Avoid infinite recursion by limiting spread iterations.

## Effects on Model Dynamics

- **Population cycles**: Stochastic events can induce or dampen cycles, making the model more closely resemble real ecosystems.
- **Extinction risk**: Small populations become more vulnerable to random shocks, demonstrating the concept of minimum viable population.
- **Genetic drift**: Random deaths can remove alleles from the gene pool, especially in small populations.
- **Resilience and adaptation**: Populations that survive repeated shocks may evolve traits that confer resistance (e.g., faster regrowth after fire, lower metabolism during drought).
- **Spatial patterns**: Localized events like fire create patchiness, leading to metapopulation dynamics.

---

## Considerations and Tips

- **Balance**: Set probabilities low enough so events are rare but impactful. Too many events can make the model chaotic and hard to analyze.
- **Calibration**: Use real ecological data if available (e.g., average fire return interval).
- **Parameter sweeping**: Use Mesa’s batch runner to explore how event frequency affects population stability.
- **Visualization**: In a GUI, you could indicate ongoing events (e.g., change background color during drought, show fire as red flashes).
- **Logging**: Record when events occur to correlate with population changes.

By incorporating stochastic events, your model gains depth and can be used to explore questions about ecosystem resilience, the role of chance in evolution, and the impact of environmental variability on species interactions.
