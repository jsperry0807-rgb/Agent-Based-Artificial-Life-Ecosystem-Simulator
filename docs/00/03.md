## Improve Model Dynamics and Behavior – Detailed Enhancements

Under this category, we focus on changing **how agents interact, move, reproduce, and respond to their environment** to create more realistic or interesting emergent patterns. Below are specific ideas you can implement, each with a brief explanation of the concept, possible implementation in Mesa, and the expected effect on the simulation.

---

### 1. Energy-Based Movement

**Concept:**  
Instead of moving one random step per turn, let rabbits decide whether to move based on their current energy level. Hungry rabbits might move less to conserve energy, or they might move more frantically in search of food. You could also tie the number of steps taken to energy reserves.

**Implementation:**
- Modify the rabbit’s `step()` method to check its energy before moving.
- If energy is very low, maybe skip movement or move only one cell.
- If energy is high, allow multiple steps (up to a maximum) or a larger jump.
- Movement cost could also scale with speed gene (e.g., faster movement costs more energy per step).

**Effect:**  
This creates a trade-off between exploration and energy conservation. Populations may develop strategies where rabbits with lower metabolism survive longer during food scarcity, while high-energy rabbits explore more.

---

### 2. Directed Movement (Foraging)

**Concept:**  
Rather than random walk, rabbits could sense nearby grass and move toward it. This introduces a simple form of intelligence and makes the model more realistic—animals don’t wander aimlessly.

**Implementation:**
- Define a vision range (e.g., 2 cells in Moore neighborhood).
- In the rabbit’s step, scan cells within vision for grass that is grown.
- If grass is found, move toward the closest or richest patch (using a gradient or random choice among neighboring cells that reduce distance).
- If no grass in sight, fall back to random movement.

**Effect:**  
Rabbits will cluster around food sources, leading to spatial patterns and more efficient foraging. This can also create competition and local overgrazing, adding new dynamics.

---

### 3. Mate-Finding and Sexual Reproduction

**Concept:**  
Instead of asexual reproduction (splitting energy), require two rabbits to be on the same cell to produce offspring. This adds a social component and can slow population growth in sparse conditions.

**Implementation:**
- Add a `gender` attribute (male/female) to rabbits.
- Modify reproduction rule: when a rabbit’s energy exceeds the threshold, it looks for a mate of the opposite gender in the same cell.
- If a mate is found, both invest energy into producing offspring (e.g., each gives a portion of energy, and offspring genes are a combination with mutation).
- If no mate, reproduction is delayed (or energy continues to accumulate).

**Effect:**  
Population dynamics become more realistic—Allee effects (low populations struggle to find mates) can emerge. Genetic diversity increases because offspring inherit from two parents.

---

### 4. Probabilistic Reproduction

**Concept:**  
Replace the deterministic threshold with a chance to reproduce each step, where probability increases with energy. This smooths out reproduction and avoids sudden bursts when energy hits a threshold.

**Implementation:**
- In rabbit step, compute `reproduction_probability = energy / some_max * base_rate`.
- Use `if self.random.random() < reproduction_probability` to decide reproduction.
- If reproduction occurs, energy is split (or a fixed cost is paid).

**Effect:**  
Reproduction becomes less predictable and more gradual, which can stabilize population cycles and reduce the risk of boom-bust patterns.

---

### 5. Aging and Lifespan

**Concept:**  
Add an age counter to each rabbit. After a maximum age (e.g., 50 steps), the rabbit dies regardless of energy. This prevents immortality and introduces generation turnover.

**Implementation:**
- In `Rabbit.__init__`, set `self.age = 0`.
- In `step()`, increment age and check `if self.age > max_age: self.remove()`.
- Max age could be a fixed parameter or also genetically influenced (with trade-offs against metabolism).

**Effect:**  
Population structure changes—older, experienced rabbits (if you add learning) might be more valuable, but they eventually die, ensuring genetic turnover. Lifespan evolution could emerge.

---

### 6. Variable Metabolism Based on Activity

**Concept:**  
Currently metabolism is a fixed cost per step. Make it depend on what the rabbit did that step: moving costs extra, eating gives a temporary boost (or reduces cost), reproducing costs extra, etc.

**Implementation:**
- In `step()`, start with a base metabolism cost.
- Add additional costs: `if moved: energy -= move_cost * gene_speed` (faster movement costs more).
- Subtract reproduction cost if offspring produced.
- Possibly reduce metabolism if the rabbit stayed still (resting).

**Effect:**  
More nuanced energy budgeting. Rabbits will need to balance foraging, moving, and resting, leading to more realistic behavioral strategies.

---

### 7. Grass Spread / Vegetative Reproduction

**Concept:**  
Allow grass to spread to neighboring cells over time, simulating clonal growth or seed dispersal. This can make the environment dynamic and prevent permanent bare patches.

**Implementation:**
- In `Grass.step()`, after regrowing, if the cell is fully grown and a random chance passes, try to place a new grass agent in a neighboring empty cell (or convert an existing grass to “grown” if already there).
- Ensure you don’t exceed one grass per cell (or allow multiple, but then eating logic must change).

**Effect:**  
The landscape becomes more resilient; overgrazed areas can recover via neighboring patches. This can create wave-like patterns of green and brown.

---

### 8. Carrying Capacity per Cell

**Concept:**  
Limit how many rabbits can occupy a single cell (or how much grass can be eaten per step). This prevents extreme crowding and overexploitation.

**Implementation:**
- When a rabbit moves into a cell, check if the number of rabbits already there exceeds a limit. If so, either block movement or impose a crowding penalty (e.g., extra energy loss due to competition).
- Similarly, grass could have a maximum “biomass” that regenerates over time, and rabbits can only eat a certain amount per step.

**Effect:**  
Prevents unrealistic stacking of rabbits on one cell and forces dispersal. Competition becomes a local phenomenon.

---

### 9. Predator-Prey Dynamics (Adding Predators)

Though listed under biological realism, adding a predator is a major behavioral change because prey will react to predators. So it fits here too.

**Concept:**  
Introduce a predator agent (e.g., fox) that hunts rabbits. Predators have their own energy, move, and reproduce. Rabbits may evolve fear responses (e.g., flee when predator nearby).

**Implementation:**
- Create `Fox` class with similar energy mechanics.
- In fox step: move toward nearby rabbits (if within vision) and eat one if on same cell.
- Rabbits could detect predators within a certain radius and move away (fleeing behavior).

**Effect:**  
Classic predator-prey cycles emerge. Selective pressure on rabbit speed and vigilance increases. Ecosystem becomes more complex.

---

### 10. Memory and Learning

**Concept:**  
Give rabbits a simple memory of where they found food recently. They can return to those locations when hungry.

**Implementation:**
- Each rabbit stores a list of recent successful foraging locations (e.g., last 5 positions where they ate grass).
- When energy drops below a threshold, instead of random move, they navigate toward one of those remembered spots.
- Memory could fade over time (forgetting).

**Effect:**  
Rabbits become more efficient foragers, leading to higher survival and potentially overexploitation of remembered patches. This could create spatial memory patterns.

---

### 11. Diurnal or Seasonal Activity Cycles

**Concept:**  
Make rabbits only active during certain times (day/night) or seasons. For example, they might rest (no movement, low metabolism) at night.

**Implementation:**
- Add a global `time_of_day` or `season` counter that toggles every N steps.
- In rabbit step, if it’s night, skip movement and reduce metabolism.
- Reproduction might only occur in certain seasons.

**Effect:**  
Adds temporal structure; populations may synchronize with environmental rhythms. Can be combined with seasonal grass growth for richer dynamics.

---

### 12. Territoriality

**Concept:**  
Rabbits may defend a home range and become aggressive toward intruders. This could be modeled by marking territory and having conflicts.

**Implementation:**
- Each rabbit has a home cell (where born) and a territory radius.
- When two rabbits meet, if one is in the other’s territory, a fight occurs (random outcome based on energy/size), loser loses energy or is displaced.

**Effect:**  
Spatial structure with exclusive ranges; reduces crowding and can lead to more even distribution.

---

### 13. Resource-Dependent Reproduction

**Concept:**  
Instead of just energy threshold, make reproduction contingent on local grass availability. Rabbits might only reproduce if the current cell has enough grass to support offspring.

**Implementation:**
- Before reproducing, check the grass in the current cell (or nearby cells) for sufficient “food security”.
- If grass is scarce, postpone reproduction even if energy is high.

**Effect:**  
Links reproduction to local environmental conditions, preventing overpopulation in poor patches.

---

### 14. Kin Selection / Altruism

**Concept:**  
Rabbits could share food with relatives or warn them of danger. This would require kinship recognition.

**Implementation:**
- Track family trees (mother/father).
- In starvation conditions, a rabbit might give some energy to a related rabbit in the same cell (if it has excess).

**Effect:**  
Adds social behavior and could lead to evolution of altruism if modeled carefully.

---

Each of these enhancements can be added incrementally. Start with one or two that interest you most, test their effects, and then combine them to see how they interact. Remember to adjust parameters to maintain balance and avoid unintended behaviors. Happy modeling!
