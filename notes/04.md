## 🐇 Realistic Rabbit Parameters

| Parameter | Realistic Value | Justification |

| **Energy from grass** | 8–10 energy units | Assume one grass bite provides enough energy for 1 day of maintenance. Rabbits need to eat several times per day, so 8–10 per bite allows them to survive on 1–2 bites/day after metabolism. |
| **Grass regrowth time** | 7–14 days | Grass in temperate regions takes 1–2 weeks to regrow after heavy grazing. |
| **Metabolism gene range** | 0.3–0.7 | Basal metabolic rate scaled so that a rabbit with gene=0.5 loses 0.5 energy/day. Combined with energy from grass, a rabbit needs to eat ~1 grass every 2 days just to break even. |
| **Reproduction threshold** | 25–30 energy | Rabbits can reproduce when they have surplus energy. In reality, a female rabbit can produce a litter every 30 days. With energy gain 8 per grass, metabolism 0.5/day, net gain ~7.5/day, it takes ~4 days to accumulate 30 energy – too fast. So you may need to **increase metabolism** or **decrease energy gain** to slow reproduction. Alternatively, model reproduction as a **probability per day** based on energy, not a fixed threshold. |

| **Senescence rate** | 0.1–0.2 | Gradual increase in mortality with age; 0.1 means metabolism increases 10% by end of life. |
| **Maximum energy** | 100–150 | A rabbit can store enough energy to survive a few days without food. |
| **Movement** | **Biased toward grass** | Rabbits should prefer cells with grass to mimic foraging. Even simple bias (e.g., 70% chance to move to a grass cell within Moore neighborhood) improves realism. |
| **Fear response** | **Avoid foxes** | If a fox is within 2 cells, rabbits move away. This creates realistic predator avoidance. |

- **Water or cover**: Not essential but could be added.

## 🔄 Suggested Code Modifications for Realism

### 1. **Sexual Reproduction**
In `Rabbit.step()` and `Fox.step()`:
- Check for another rabbit/fox of the opposite sex on the same cell.
- If both have energy ≥ threshold, create multiple offspring (litter size 1–8 for rabbits, 4–6 for foxes).
- Reduce parents' energy by a fixed cost (not half).

### 2. **Vision and Directed Movement**
Add a `sense_radius` attribute (e.g., 3 for rabbits, 5 for foxes).  
- **Rabbits**: Look for grass cells within radius; move toward one if found, else random. Also look for foxes; move away if detected.
- **Foxes**: Look for rabbits; move toward the closest, else random. Optionally, avoid other foxes (territoriality).

### 3. **Multiple Steps per Day**
If each step is a day, movement of 1 cell is too slow. Either increase grid cell size conceptually (e.g., each cell = 50 m) or allow agents to move multiple cells per step (e.g., `step_size = 2` for rabbits, `step_size = 3` for foxes).

### 4. **Starvation and Energy**
Ensure metabolism is high enough that animals must eat regularly. With your current ranges, animals can survive many days without food, which is unrealistic. Increase metabolism or decrease energy values.
